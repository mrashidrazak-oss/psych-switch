What it does

Stop reverse-engineering Maudsley tables at the bedside. PsychSwitch produces an evidence-graded schedule, adapted to your patient's actual doses, with the source citation one tap away.

Adaptive scaling

The reviewed schedule is a template. Enter your patient's actual doses; the engine scales every step, rounds to formulation, caps at clinical max, and merges duplicate rounding.

Smart drug picker

Once you've picked the FROM drug, the TO list re-orders by clinical relevance — reviewed pairs first, contraindications last. Patient-context-aware.

Citation chips

Every step has a tappable chip pointing at the Maudsley page or BAP section it came from. Tap to see the paraphrased quote in under 2 seconds.

PsychSwitch Score

One 0-100 number summarising evidence grade, AE alignment, patient-context safety, DDI severity and dose fidelity. Tap for the breakdown.

Workflow loop

Discharge summary block ready to paste into the EMR. Plain-language patient counselling card. Real PDF export. All from one engine output.

Predicted side-effects

For the target drug, see likelihood tiers for akathisia, weight gain, prolactin, QTc and more — including "lower than current" comparative hints.

Auto-monitoring schedule

Lithium → U&E q3mo. Clozapine → weekly FBC ×18. Auto-derived from drug + patient context. Day-by-day checklist.

Privacy-first

Patient context, saved cases, sign-offs all local. No analytics. No tracking. Nothing leaves the device unless you tap Share.

Built by clinicians, for clinicians

PsychSwitch is built and maintained by Dr Rashid Razak, consultant psychiatrist. Every reviewed rule is signed off and re-reviewed on a 90-day cadence. The clinical content is open under CC BY-NC-SA — anyone can submit a correction via GitHub PR or the in-app errata button.

Decision-support, not medical advice. Cross-titration schedules, monitoring plans, equivalency tables and adverse-effect mappings are reference content drawn from published guidelines. The treating clinician's judgement always takes precedence.
